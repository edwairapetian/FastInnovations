\section{Extensions}\label{app:extensions}

\subsection{Fertility depletion}\label{app:fertility}

Suppose topics also differ in fertility, and later topics are less fertile.
The $m$-th topic opened has fertility $f(m)=\bar f\,m^{-\eta_f}$, so members of
topic $m$ earn $a f(m)$ times the payoff curve. The value of exploring falls
with the number of open topics at elasticity $\eta_a$, which captures
\emph{access depletion}: the $\eta_F$ of the main text. A successful explorer
opens a topic of fertility $f(M)$. Newcomers are indifferent between topic $m$
and exploring when
\[
f(m)\,w(P_m)=F(M)f(M)\iff w(P_t)=\bar F\,M^{-\eta_a}\,t^{\eta_f},\qquad t\equiv m/M\in(0,1],
\]
where for simplicity founders' payoffs are absorbed into $\bar F$ ($\theta_F=0$).
\begin{proposition}[Scale invariance under fertility depletion]
(i) If $\eta_a=0$, the distribution of densities across topics, and hence of
overlap rates and aware shares, is invariant to $N$ and $a$, whatever the value of
$\eta_f$. (ii) In general, with $\mathcal D\equiv\dd\ln M^*/\dd\ln N$,
\[
\lambda_J=1-(\eta_a+\eta_f)\,\mathcal D,\qquad
\mathcal D=\Big(1+\eta_a\,\E_n\!\Big[\frac{D(P_t)}{|\eps_w(P_t)|}\Big]\Big)^{-1},
\]
where $\E_n$ averages over topics with weights proportional to members. The
paper-weighted volume elasticity of density identifies $\eta_a$, not $\eta_f$, and
$\lambda_J\le1-\eta_a\mathcal D$, the value obtained when $\eta_f=0$. With a single
density, $1-\eta_a\mathcal D=1-\mathcal E|\eps_w|$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) With $\eta_a=0$ the indifference condition pins $P_t$ as a function of the
relative rank $t$ alone. Members per topic $n(P_t)$ are then invariant, so
$M=N/\int_0^1n(P_t)\dd t$ scales with $N$. (ii) Every researcher's career payoff is
$aF(M)f(M)$, so $Y=Na\bar F\bar f M^{-\eta_a-\eta_f}$ and
$\lambda_J=1-(\eta_a+\eta_f)\mathcal D$. At a fixed rank, $\eps_w(P_t)\,\dd\ln
P_t=-\eta_a\,\dd\ln M$. Substituting into $N=M\int_0^1n(P_t)\dd t$ gives
$\dd\ln N=\dd\ln M\,(1+\eta_a\E_n[D/|\eps_w|])$.
\end{proof}

Fertility depletion, the textbook ``fishing out'' of ideas \citep{kortum1997}, thus
leaves the anchor result intact and strengthens it: volume opens new topics
exactly. It adds to stepping on toes a component that density data cannot
see. This is why \cref{prop:sot} delivers an upper bound on $\lambda_J$.

\subsection{Fertility heterogeneity and the cross-section}

With fertility $\zeta$ drawn independently at a topic's birth, a mature topic
solves $\zeta w(P^m)=F$. More fertile topics are denser, their incumbents earn
more, and the cross-sectional returns gradient is $\eps_\Gamma(P^m)$
(\cref{prop:mature}(iv)). The most fertile topics sit closest to the speed
limit (\cref{thm:speed}). Topics with $\zeta\hat w<F$ cannot sustain
replacement entry. They shrink once their growth spurt ends and are eventually
abandoned, like topics that grow too slowly to take off (\cref{prop:toofast}).

\subsection{Nested topics and the radius pattern}\label{app:nested}

Fact~\ref{fact:volume} finds that the volume elasticity of density rises with
the similarity radius: it is close to zero at the radius of close overlap and
larger at broad radii. The model's topic is a neighbourhood of fixed size
(\cref{rem:breadth}), and a two-scale version delivers the pattern. Let idea
space consist of \emph{fields} of fixed breadth, each containing topic cells.
A researcher can join an open topic, open a new topic inside an existing field,
or open a new field. Opening a topic inside a field is worth
$F_T(m)=\bar F_T\,m^{-\eta_T}$, where $m$ is the number of open topics per field,
and opening a field is worth $F_B(M_B)=\bar F_B\,M_B^{-\eta_B}$, where $M_B$ is
the number of fields. Learning and covering act within topics.

\begin{proposition}[The radius pattern]\label{prop:nested}
In the large-topic limit, the volume elasticity of density at the topic scale is
\[
\mathcal E_T\equiv\frac{\dd\ln P^*}{\dd\ln N}=\frac{1}{D+|\eps_w|\,(1/\eta_T+1/\eta_B)},
\]
and the volume elasticity of density at the field scale, papers per unit of
field breadth $mP^*$, is
\[
\mathcal E_B\equiv\frac{\dd\ln(m^*P^*)}{\dd\ln N}=\mathcal E_T\Big(1+\frac{|\eps_w|}{\eta_T}\Big)\ge\mathcal E_T .
\]
If topics are easy to open inside fields and fields hard to open
($\eta_T\ll\eta_B$), $\mathcal E_T$ is close to zero while $\mathcal E_B$ is close
to $1/(1+\eta_T/\eta_B)$: close overlap is pinned while broad neighbourhoods
fill. With neither margin open ($\eta_T,\eta_B\to\infty$) both equal $1/D$, the
fixed-field benchmark.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Indifference among the three options gives $w(P)=F_T(m)=F_B(M_B)$ and the
adding-up condition is $N=M_B\,m\,n(P)$. Log-differentiating,
$\eps_w\,\dd\ln P=-\eta_T\,\dd\ln m=-\eta_B\,\dd\ln M_B$ and
$\dd\ln N=\dd\ln M_B+\dd\ln m+D\,\dd\ln P$. Substituting
$\dd\ln m=(|\eps_w|/\eta_T)\,\dd\ln P$ and $\dd\ln M_B=(|\eps_w|/\eta_B)\,\dd\ln P$
gives $\mathcal E_T$, and $\dd\ln(mP)=(1+|\eps_w|/\eta_T)\,\dd\ln P$ gives
$\mathcal E_B$. The limits follow directly.
\end{proof}

The proposition turns the radius pattern of Fact~\ref{fact:volume} into a
prediction and a measurement. The ratio of the broad-radius to the fine-radius
elasticity identifies $1+|\eps_w|/\eta_T$, the ease of opening topics within
existing fields. The ambitious version of this extension is a continuous idea
space with a wide learning kernel and a narrow covering kernel, in which
topics form endogenously as in the spatial models of
\citet{lucasrossihansberg2002} and \citet{allenarkolakis2014}. A wide learning
kernel adds a spillover from neighbouring topics, which makes opening a topic
next to existing ones more attractive than opening one in empty space, and so
raises $\mathcal E_B$ relative to $\mathcal E_T$. We leave that version to future
work.

\subsection{Learning from the stock}\label{app:stock}

In the main text learning draws on the flow and catch-up on the stock. Let
learning instead draw on the effective literature
$L=(1-\chi)P+\chi\,\delta S$, with $\chi\in[0,1]$, while covering papers still
arrive with the flow. In a stationary topic $\delta S=P$, so $L=P$, and every
stationary result is unchanged: the payoff curve, the mature density, the
speed limit and the incidence of tools. Along a growth path the stock lags, so
$L<P$ and a young topic offers less to learn from than a mature topic of the
same density. Two things follow. A newborn topic, which has no stock, offers
its first follower $v$ evaluated at $L=(1-\chi)P_0$, which is below $v(P_0)$; the
condition for newborn topics to attract followers tightens. In the
calibration it fails for every $\chi$ above \calChiBar: if more than that share
of learning drew on the accumulated stock, no new topic would ever find a
follower. Below that bound young topics are less attractive, so they need to
grow faster to take off and to overshoot. With $\chi=\calChiHalf$ the take-off
threshold rises from \calGtakeoff\ to \calGtakeoffChiHalf\ and the overshooting
threshold from \calGstarExact\ to \calGstarChiHalf. Learning from the flow is
therefore not a convenience: it is what lets topics be born.

\subsection{Search for overlap on every project}\label{app:perproject}

In the main text a researcher's reading protects all her projects at once.
Suppose instead that overlap must be checked project by project, as our
evidence on targeted search suggests. Each project takes $1/a$ of production
time plus a search time $t$ in which the researcher screens $b_st$ papers of her
topic's literature, so that each earlier covering paper is caught with
probability $\varphi(b_st/P)$. Learning still comes from the reading share $r$.
Her payoff per period is
\[
a\,\frac{(1-r)\,Q(br,P)}{1+a\,t}\,\exp\Big\{-\theta P\big[\lambda-\Delta\varphi(b_st/P)\big]-\theta_c\lambda_cP\Big\}.
\]
Let $\varsigma\equiv at/(1+at)$ be the share of project time spent searching.

\begin{proposition}[Per-project search]\label{prop:perproject}
(i) At a fixed density, a production speed-up raises the payoff by $(1-\varsigma)\,\dd\ln a$ and a search speed-up by $\varsigma\,\dd\ln b_s$: per unit of time saved, every speed-up is still worth the same.
(ii) Faster production lowers the papers screened per project, $b_st$, and so raises the share of earlier covering papers that go undetected.
(iii) Production speed no longer drops out of the payoff curve: $v$ depends on $a$ through $\varsigma$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) By the envelope theorem, $\partial\ln(\text{payoff})/\partial\ln a=1-at/(1+at)$
at the optimal $t$, and $\partial/\partial\ln b_s=\theta\Delta\varphi'(c_s)c_s$ with
$c_s=b_st/P$, which the first-order condition for $t$,
$\theta\Delta\varphi'(c_s)\,b_s/P=a/(1+at)$, equates to $at/(1+at)$. Time saved per
project is $(1/a)\dd\ln a$ and $t\,\dd\ln b_s$ respectively, and both are valued
at the same shadow price. (ii) Write the first-order condition as
$G(t,a)\equiv\theta\Delta\varphi'(b_st/P)\,b_s/P-a/(1+at)=0$. Then
$\partial G/\partial a=-1/(1+at)^2<0$, and $\partial G/\partial t<0$ by the
second-order condition, so $\dd t/\dd a=-(\partial G/\partial a)/(\partial G/\partial t)<0$:
search per project falls, and with it detection. (iii) follows from (i).
\end{proof}

Per-project search narrows the gap between production and reading tools at
fixed topics, since production is only a share $1-\varsigma$ of a project, and it
makes faster production raise the rate of unaware duplicates. The incidence
results of \cref{sec:ai} are unchanged in form: what matters for the long run
is still how a tool moves the frontier relative to existing topics.

\subsection{Heterogeneous ability}

\citet{ekerdtwu2024} attribute part of measured stepping on toes to falling
researcher ability. Let ability $z$ multiply a researcher's credit. If ability
and reading are complements ($Q$ increasing in $z\,R$), able researchers gain
more from dense literatures and sort into them. If ability also raises the
explorer's founding rate, they sort to the frontier, where they also raise
$\theta_F$. Which force wins determines whether the dense-topic premium in
Fact~\ref{fact:hump} partly reflects sorting. Author fixed effects remove level
differences in ability but not ability-specific returns to density. The two
can be separated by interacting density with pre-period ability (early-career
citations), which we leave for future work.

\subsection{Credit rules}

Partial credit enters only through $\Theta=\theta\lambda$, $\Delta/\lambda$ and
$\Theta_c=\theta_c\lambda_c$. \Cref{thm:v} requires $\lambda>\ell$ and nothing else.
The sign of the externality at a stable equilibrium
(\cref{prop:eq,cor:overcrowd}) therefore survives whenever duplicates earn some
credit. Concurrent overlap adds a term linear in density that no reading
prevents: it steepens the fall of $v$ at high density without changing what
reading is worth for protection. Replication value can be added as a positive
social value of aware follow-ons that is not credited privately. This raises
the social value of detection relative to the private one, but it does not
change the sign of $w'$ at equilibrium.

\subsection{Transitional dynamics after a production shock}\label{app:transition}

A one-time increase in $a$ raises every topic's flow immediately, before
membership adjusts. Topics jump to the right along $v$, overlap rises, and
incumbents' payoffs change by $\eps_v\,\dd\ln a$. The newcomer's payoff falls, by
$|\eps_w|\,\dd\ln a$ at the old membership, so newcomers go to the frontier and
topics thin through turnover, returning towards $P^*$ at rate $\rho$. Topics
that the shock pushes past the speed limit close entirely until their flow
falls back below $b_c$ (\cref{thm:speed}(iv)). In the calibration, closing even
the densest mature topics requires production speed to rise by a factor of
\calCloseAlpha; a fourfold rise closes topics that hold \calCloseFour\ of
mature researcher-years. The overlap rate spikes and decays with half-life
$\ln2/\rho$. Papers per author rise immediately and stay higher. The
author-level estimate of Fact~\ref{fact:authors}, about \empLLMallPct\ more
papers for adopters, bounds the size of the production shock so far. With adopters a minority of
active submitters (about one in fifteen in our classification), the aggregate
increase in effective production speed is at most a few percent, too small to
produce a visible spike in overlap or to close any topic.

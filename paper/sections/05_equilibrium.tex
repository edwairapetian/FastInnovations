\section{Free topic choice without frictions}\label{sec:frictionless}

We first set $\kappa=0$, so $w=v$. Newcomers move freely between topics and the
frontier. This benchmark isolates the topic-choice margin and reproduces the
draft's core claims, now as theorems.

\begin{proposition}[Equilibrium and dissipation]\label{prop:eq}
Let \cref{ass:Q,ass:phi,ass:credit,ass:interior} hold, $\kappa=0$, and suppose
$\vhat>F(N/n(\Phat))$. Then a topic equilibrium exists and is unique. Every open
topic sits on the falling side of the payoff curve, $P^*>\Phat$, where an
additional paper lowers the payoff of every member (\cref{prop:externality}).
Payoff per researcher equals the frontier payoff $a\,F(M^*)<a\,\vhat$. The gain
from cumulative knowledge, $\vhat-F(M^*)$, is competed away.
\end{proposition}

The logic is that of the open-access fishery \citep{gordon1954,weitzman1974},
with one difference: the resource is attention to the literature, and the
people who crowd it are the same people who must read it. Entry continues past
the peak because a newcomer compares the \emph{average} payoff in a topic with
the frontier. She ignores that her papers lower everyone else's payoff once
the topic is past $\Phat$. Crossings on the rising side are unstable: one more
entrant raises the payoff of entering. So topics fill past the peak, to the
crossing on the falling side.

\paragraph{Volume opens new topics.} The next result is the paper's anchor. It
says how a larger community distributes itself.

\begin{proposition}[Volume opens new topics]\label{prop:volume}
In the large-topic limit, equilibrium density depends on $(N,a)$ only through
the product $N a$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E}
\mathcal E\equiv\frac{\dd\ln P^*}{\dd\ln N}=\frac{\eta}{\eta\,D+|\eps_w|},\qquad D\equiv1-\eps_x+\kappa P^*\ge 1,
\end{equation}
where $\eps_w=\dd\ln w/\dd\ln P<0$ and $\eps_x=\dd\ln x^*/\dd\ln P\le 0$ are evaluated at $P^*$.
New topics absorb a share $\dd\ln M^*/\dd\ln N=1-D\,\mathcal E=|\eps_w|/(\eta D+|\eps_w|)$ of growth, and papers per researcher move with elasticity $\eps_x\mathcal E\le 0$.
If the frontier is perfectly elastic ($\eta=0$), density, the overlap rate and the aware share do not depend on $N$, $a$ or the cost of entering science.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{eq:E} holds for $\kappa\ge0$; we use it again in \cref{sec:catchup}. Three
remarks.

First, the overlap rate is \emph{pinned by entry, not by volume}. When the community grows,
newcomers open new topics until existing ones are no more attractive than the
frontier. The flagged overlap rate and the aware share are functions of $P^*$
alone (\cref{lem:credit}), so they inherit its invariance. A flat overlap rate
while submissions double (Fact~\ref{fact:volume}) is what the model predicts. It is not evidence that
crowding is harmless.

Second, a fixed field corresponds to $\eta\to\infty$. Then $\mathcal E\to1/D$:
growth thickens existing topics. The arXiv elasticity of 0.04--0.27, against a
fixed-field benchmark of 0.53--0.89, implies that roughly three quarters of
growth opens new ground. Inverting \eqref{eq:E},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
\eta=\frac{\mathcal E\,|\eps_w|}{1-\mathcal E\,D},
\end{equation}
so the frontier elasticity is identified by the volume elasticity and the
slope of the entrant's payoff curve (\cref{fig:planner}, right).

Third, papers per author barely move when the community grows, because $\eps_x\mathcal E$
is the product of two small numbers. Growth through new authors with flat
papers per author (Fact~\ref{fact:authors}) is the signature of cheaper entry, not of faster
drafting (\cref{sec:ai}).

\begin{remark}[Reading capacity per researcher or per field]
The draft discussed a variant with a fixed screening capacity $B$ per field, so
that coverage is $B/P$ and researchers write full time. Topic choice still
pins density, so \cref{prop:eq,prop:volume} survive. But if topics cannot
split, overlap rises with field volume, which the arXiv volume test rejects.
Per-researcher capacity also keeps the time-allocation margin that separates
writing tools from reading tools (\cref{sec:ai}). We adopt it throughout.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The planner.} A planner who controls how many researchers join each
topic, but not how they split their time, maximises
$Y=a\,[f_0M+M\,n\,w(P)]$ subject to $\Phi(M)+Mn=N$.

\begin{proposition}[Overcrowding and the frontier subsidy]\label{prop:planner}
Let the conditions of \cref{prop:eq} hold, with $\kappa\ge0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The planner's density $P^o$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plannerfoc}
\frac{w(P^o)-F(M^o)}{w(P^o)}=|\eps_w(P^o)|\,\frac{1+n(P^o)\,\pi(M^o)}{D(P^o)},
\end{equation}
with $\hat P_w<P^o<P^*$ and $M^o>M^*$, where $\hat P_w$ is the peak of $w$. The planner opens more topics and puts fewer researchers in each. Overlap stays positive at the optimum. It is lower than in equilibrium whenever the credit lost to overlap rises with density, as it does in every calibration we report.
\item In the large-topic limit $P^o\to\hat P_w$. Topics sit at the peak of the entrant's payoff curve, which is the peak of $v$ when $\kappa=0$.
\item A frontier subsidy (equivalently, a tax on joining open topics) of $s^o=w(P^o)-F(M^o)$ per unit of writing speed decentralises the planner's allocation.
\item (Two wedges) A planner who also chooses $x$ wants researchers to write less than $x^*(P)$ wherever $w'(P)<0$ and more wherever $w'(P)>0$. The writing wedge is proportional to $(\ln w)'(P)$. In the large-topic limit it vanishes at the optimal density, so controlling topic choice alone attains the first best.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

There are two wedges, and they differ in importance. Newcomers choose topics
on the average payoff while the planner uses the marginal one: this is the
\emph{crowding wedge}. Inside each topic, researchers ignore that their writing adds to what
colleagues must read: this is the \emph{writing wedge}. Part (iv) shows the
writing wedge is a symptom of the crowding wedge. Once topics are at their best density,
researchers' private time allocation is efficient. Policies that work only
inside topics are dissipated: writing caps, reading subsidies, better tools.
With $\eta=0$ the equilibrium payoff stays pinned at $aF$ whatever happens
inside topics. Policies that change the frontier are not dissipated: frontier
grants, credit for first papers in a cluster, prizes for opening fields.
Fund the frontier, not the reading tools.

\section{The payoff curve}\label{sec:payoff}

Every result in the paper rests on the shape of $v$. The draft version of this
model could establish that shape only in examples. The next theorem shows it holds for
every learning and detection technology that satisfies
\cref{ass:Q,ass:phi,ass:credit}.

\begin{assumption}[Interior reading and learning potential]\label{ass:interior}
(a) Reading the first paper is worth its time: $b\,[\partial_R\ln Q(0,P)+\theta\Delta\varphi'(0)]>1$ for all $P>0$.
(b) A thin literature is worth reading: for some $\varrho>0$,
\[
\nu_0(\varrho)\equiv\lim_{P\downarrow 0}\frac{Q(\varrho P,P)-1}{P}-\frac{\varrho}{b}-\theta\big[\lambda-\Delta\varphi(\varrho)\big]>0.
\]
\end{assumption}

Part (b) compares, at the first papers of a topic, the gain from learning along the
ray $R=\varrho P$ with the time spent reading and the overlap risk. In the
example it reads $\sup_\varrho\{\gamma\beta\varrho/(1+\varrho)-\varrho/b-\theta[\lambda-\Delta(1-e^{-\sigma\varrho})]\}>0$.

\begin{theorem}[The payoff curve]\label{thm:v}
Under \cref{ass:Q,ass:phi,ass:credit}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $v$ is continuous on $\R_+$, $v(0)=1$, $v(P)\ge e^{-\theta\lambda P}>0$ and $\lim_{P\to\infty}v(P)=0$.
\item $\ln v$ is concave on $(0,\infty)$. Under \cref{ass:interior}(a) it is strictly concave.
\item Under \cref{ass:interior}, there is a unique $\Phat\in(0,\infty)$ such that $v$ is strictly increasing on $[0,\Phat]$ and strictly decreasing on $[\Phat,\infty)$, with $\vhat\equiv v(\Phat)>1$. For every $F\in(0,\vhat)$ the equation $v(P)=F$ has exactly one root above $\Phat$.
\item (Envelope) $v$ is differentiable on $(0,\infty)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
\frac{v'(P)}{v(P)}=\underbrace{\partial_P\ln Q(R^*,P)}_{\text{more to learn from}}\;-\;\underbrace{\theta\big[\lambda-\Delta\big(\varphi(c^*)-c^*\varphi'(c^*)\big)\big]}_{\text{more to miss}},
\end{equation}
where $R^*=b\,r^*(P)$ and $c^*=R^*/P$.
\item The optimal reading share $r^*(P)$ is unique and nondecreasing in $P$. Papers per researcher, $a\,x^*(P)$, are nonincreasing in density.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof (\cref{app:proofs}) rests on one observation. $\ln g$ is
\emph{jointly} concave in reading time and density. $\ln(1-r)$ and $\ln Q$ are
concave by assumption. The erosion exponent $-\theta\lambda P+\theta\Delta\,P\varphi(R/P)$
is linear plus the perspective of the concave function $\varphi$, and
perspectives preserve concavity. Maximising a jointly concave function over
one argument leaves a concave function of the other, so $\ln v$ is concave. A
log-concave function that starts at one, rises, and vanishes at infinity is
single peaked. Part (v) follows from Topkis's theorem: $\partial^2\ln
C/\partial R\,\partial P=-\theta\Delta\varphi''(c)\,c/P\ge 0$ and $\ln Q$ is
supermodular, so reading and density are complements.

The theorem answers the draft's first open problem (``find conditions on $Q$
and $\varphi$ that guarantee single-peakedness''). It needs no parametric
assumption, and the conditions are economic: no increasing returns to reading,
and diminishing returns to search. Note what the theorem does \emph{not} need.
Duplicates may keep most of their credit, since only $\lambda>\ell$ is
required. The single peak comes from overlap that goes unread, not from an
assumption that overlap is worthless.

\paragraph{The sign of the spillover.} Let one more paper appear in a topic. By the
envelope theorem, the change in every other member's payoff is exactly
$a\,v'(P)$. The researchers' own reallocation of time is second order.

\begin{proposition}[Spillover sign]\label{prop:externality}
An additional paper in a topic of density $P$ changes the per-period payoff of
each incumbent by $a\,v'(P)$. The change is positive for $P<\Phat$ (more to
learn from) and negative for $P>\Phat$ (more to miss).
\end{proposition}

The sign of the spillover is therefore \emph{derived}, not assumed. A paper
helps its topic when the topic is thin and hurts it when the topic is dense.
Overproduction is not built in. Whether science is overcrowded depends on
where equilibrium places topics on $v$. That is the subject of the next two
sections.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

Throughout, ``concave'' means concave on the stated convex domain, and
$\partial_R$, $\partial_P$ denote partial derivatives.

\paragraph{The example satisfies \cref{ass:Q,ass:phi}.} The function
$h(R,P)=RP/(R+P)$ is half the harmonic mean of $R$ and $P$. It is concave,
nondecreasing and homogeneous of degree one on $\R_+^2$, with $h\le\min\{R,P\}$.
So $\ln(1+\beta h)$ is concave as a nondecreasing concave transformation of a
concave function, and $Q$ is bounded by $(1+\beta R)^\gamma$ for fixed $R$.
Supermodularity: $\partial_R\ln Q=\gamma\beta h_R/(1+\beta h)$ with
$h_R=P^2/(R+P)^2$ and $h_{RP}=2RP/(R+P)^3$, so
\[
\partial_P\partial_R\ln Q=\gamma\beta\,\frac{h_{RP}(1+\beta h)-\beta h_Rh_P}{(1+\beta h)^2}
=\gamma\beta\,\frac{2RP(R+P)+\beta R^2P^2}{(R+P)^4(1+\beta h)^2}\ge0 .
\]
For $\varphi(c)=1-e^{-\sigma c}$: $\varphi(0)=0$, $\varphi'=\sigma e^{-\sigma c}>0$, $\varphi''<0$.

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{lem:credit}.} Earlier covering papers form a Poisson
count $K$ with mean $\theta P$. Each is detected independently with
probability $\varphi$, so detected and undetected counts $K_d,K_u$ are
independent Poisson with means $\theta P\varphi$ and $\theta P(1-\varphi)$.
Concurrent covering projects form an independent Poisson count $K_c$ with mean
$\theta_cP$. Credit is the product of $(1-\lambda)$ over undetected papers, of
independent factors with mean $1-\ell$ over detected papers, and of
$(1-\lambda_c)$ over concurrent projects. Using $\E[t^{K}]=e^{-\mu(1-t)}$,
\[
C=\E\big[(1-\lambda)^{K_u}\big]\,\E\big[(1-\ell)^{K_d}\big]\,\E\big[(1-\lambda_c)^{K_c}\big]
=\exp\{-\theta P[\lambda-(\lambda-\ell)\varphi]-\theta_c\lambda_cP\}.
\]
Flagged overlap consists of undetected covering papers (rate $\theta
P(1-\varphi)$) and detected ones that proceed (rate $\theta P\varphi q$). The
aware share is the ratio of the second rate to the sum. Proceeding happens iff
$\delta>1-\kappa_p$, which gives the conditional mean. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{lem:career}.} With a constant flow, \eqref{eq:backlog}
is linear with rest point $B^\star=(P-b_c)/\delta$, so
$B(t)=B^\star+(S-B^\star)e^{-\delta t}$. If $P<b_c$, $B^\star<0$ and $B$ reaches
zero at $e^{-\delta T}=-B^\star/(S-B^\star)=(b_c-P)/(\delta S+b_c-P)$, which is
\eqref{eq:T}. If $P>b_c$, $B(t)$ converges to $B^\star>0$ from $S>0$ and never
reaches zero; if $P=b_c$, $B(t)=Se^{-\delta t}>0$. With $S=P/\delta$,
$1+\delta S/(b_c-P)=b_c/(b_c-P)$, which gives the stationary $T$. A newcomer's
spell in the topic ends at rate $\rho+\xi$ (her career ends or the topic dies).
Her expected output over the spell divided by its expected length is
$(\rho+\xi)\int_T^\infty e^{-(\rho+\xi)t}a\,v(P)\,\dd t=a\,v(P)e^{-(\rho+\xi)T}$, and
$e^{-(\rho+\xi)T}=(1-P/b_c)^{(\rho+\xi)/\delta}$. In a stationary topic with
entry flow $\iota$, members who joined $u$ periods ago are still there with
probability $e^{-\rho u}$ while the topic lives, so the mass of members is
$\iota/\rho$ and the mass that has caught up is $\iota e^{-\rho T}/\rho$. The
producing share is $e^{-\rho T}=(1-P/b_c)^{\rho/\delta}$, and \eqref{eq:flow} gives
$P=a\,x^*(P)\,n\,(1-P/b_c)^{\rho/\delta}$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{lem:frontier}.} Value an option by the ratio of its
expected output to its expected length (renewal reward). The exploring phase
ends at rate $\rho+s$ (the career ends, or she founds a topic) and produces $e$
per period. With probability $s/(\rho+s)$ it is followed by a founding phase that
ends at rate $\rho+\xi$ and produces $\bar U_F/(\rho+\xi)$ in expectation, by the
definition of $\bar U_F$. Expected output is
$[e+s\,\bar U_F/(\rho+\xi)]/(\rho+s)$ and expected length is
$[1+s/(\rho+\xi)]/(\rho+s)$. Their ratio is
$[(\rho+\xi)e+s\bar U_F]/(\rho+\xi+s)=(1-\omega)e+\omega\bar U_F$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{lem:little}.} Each researcher's career is a sequence of
spells, each begun by a choice among options and ended by the end of her
career, the death of her topic or, for explorers, the founding of a topic.
By the renewal-reward theorem, her long-run output per period is the ratio of
expected output to expected length of the spells she chooses, which is $aF$
for every option she takes. In a stationary population of independent
researchers the cross-section average of output per period equals this time
average. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{thm:v}.}
\emph{(i)} Since $Q(0,P)=1$ and $\varphi(0)=0$, $v(P)\ge g(0,P)=e^{-(\theta\lambda+\theta_c\lambda_c)P}$. Because $Q$ and $\varphi$ are nondecreasing in $R$ and $R\le b$,
\[
g(r,P)\le \bar Q(b)\exp\{-\theta P[\lambda-\Delta\varphi(b/P)]\}\xrightarrow[P\to\infty]{}0,
\]
since $\varphi(b/P)\to0$ and $\lambda>0$. At $P=0$, $g(r,0)=1-r$, so $v(0)=1$. Define $\ln C(R,0)=0$. Then $|\theta P\varphi(R/P)|\le\theta P$ gives joint continuity of $g$ on $[0,1]\times\R_+$ ($g\to0$ as $r\to1$). Berge's maximum theorem gives continuity of $v$.

\emph{(ii)} On $D=[0,1)\times(0,\infty)$,
\[
\ln g(r,P)=\ln(1-r)+\ln Q(br,P)-(\theta\lambda+\theta_c\lambda_c) P+\theta\Delta\,P\varphi\!\left(\frac{br}{P}\right).
\]
The first term is concave in $r$. The second is concave by \cref{ass:Q}
composed with a linear map. The third is linear. In the fourth, $(R,P)\mapsto
P\varphi(R/P)$ is the perspective of the concave function $\varphi$, which is
jointly concave on $\R\times\R_{++}$ \citep[\S3.2.6]{boydvandenberghe2004}. So
$\ln g$ is jointly concave on $D$. Since $\ln g\to-\infty$ as $r\to1$, the
supremum over $r$ is attained in $[0,1)$. Partial maximisation of a jointly
concave function over a convex set preserves concavity
\citep[\S3.2.5]{boydvandenberghe2004}. Hence $\ln v(P)=\max_r\ln g(r,P)$ is concave.

For strict concavity, take $P_1\neq P_2$ with maximisers $r_1,r_2$ and $t\in(0,1)$. Under \cref{ass:interior}(a), $\partial_r\ln g(0,P)>0$, so $r_i>0$. If $r_1\neq r_2$, strict concavity of $\ln(1-r)$ gives
$\ln g(r_t,P_t)>t\ln g(r_1,P_1)+(1-t)\ln g(r_2,P_2)$. If $r_1=r_2=r>0$, then $P\mapsto P\varphi(br/P)$ has second derivative $\varphi''(br/P)(br)^2/P^3<0$, which gives the same strict inequality. In either case $\ln v(P_t)\ge\ln g(r_t,P_t)>t\ln v(P_1)+(1-t)\ln v(P_2)$.

\emph{(iii)} Fix $\varrho$ with $\nu_0(\varrho)>0$. For small $P$, $r=\varrho P/b$ is feasible and
\[
\frac{v(P)-1}{P}\ge\frac{g(\varrho P/b,P)-1}{P}
=\frac{(1-\varrho P/b)\,Q(\varrho P,P)\,e^{-\theta P[\lambda-\Delta\varphi(\varrho)]-\theta_c\lambda_cP}-1}{P}\longrightarrow \nu_0(\varrho)>0 .
\]
So $v>1=v(0)$ on some interval $(0,\epsilon)$. A strictly log-concave function is strictly quasi-concave, so its maximiser is unique. It is positive because $v$ exceeds $v(0)$ near zero, and finite because $v\to0$. Strict quasi-concavity gives strict monotonicity on either side of $\Phat$. Since $v$ falls continuously and strictly from $\vhat$ to $0$ on $[\Phat,\infty)$, each $F\in(0,\vhat)$ has exactly one root there.

\emph{(iv)} For $P>0$ the maximiser $r^*(P)$ is unique (strict concavity in $r$) and $\partial_P g$ is continuous. The envelope theorem \citep{milgromsegal2002} gives $v'(P)=\partial_Pg(r^*(P),P)$. Differentiating $\ln C$,
\[
\partial_P\big[-\theta\lambda P+\theta\Delta P\varphi(R/P)-\theta_c\lambda_cP\big]=-\theta\lambda+\theta\Delta\big[\varphi(c)-c\varphi'(c)\big]-\theta_c\lambda_c,\qquad c=R/P,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:envelope}. Concavity and $\varphi(0)=0$ give $\varphi(c)-c\varphi'(c)\ge0$.

\emph{(v)} $\partial_r\partial_P\ln g=b\,[\partial_R\partial_P\ln Q+\partial_R\partial_P\ln C]$. The first term is nonnegative by \cref{ass:Q}. For the second,
$\partial_R\ln C=\theta\Delta\varphi'(R/P)$, so $\partial_P\partial_R\ln C=-\theta\Delta\varphi''(c)\,c/P\ge0$. So $\ln g$ has increasing differences in $(r,P)$, and the unique maximiser is nondecreasing by Topkis's monotonicity theorem \citep{topkis1998}. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{lem:timeshares}.} $b$ enters $g$ only through
$R=br$, so by the envelope theorem
$\partial\ln v/\partial\ln b=b\,r\,\partial_R\ln(QC)$ at $r^*$. At an interior
optimum the first-order condition is $b\,\partial_R\ln(QC)=1/(1-r)$, so
$\partial\ln v/\partial\ln b=r^*/(1-r^*)$; at a corner $r^*=0$ both sides are
zero. A researcher's payoff is $a\,v(P)$, so production speed raises it by
$\dd\ln a$. A speed-up by $\dd\ln(\text{speed})$ of reading saves
$r^*\dd\ln b$ of her time and of production saves $x^*\dd\ln a$, which gives
the value per unit of time saved, $1/x^*$, in both cases. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:externality}.} By \cref{thm:v}(iv), the effect
of a marginal paper on an incumbent's per-period payoff is $a\,v'(P)$. Its sign is
that of $\Phat-P$ by \cref{thm:v}(iii). \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:eq}.} With $b_c=\infty$, $n(P)=P/(a x^*(P))$ is
continuous and strictly increasing, since $x^*$ is nonincreasing by
\cref{thm:v}(v). Let $M(P)$ solve $M\,(n(P)+\xi/s)=N$. It is unique, continuous
and strictly decreasing in $P$. Define $\Psi(P)=\ln v(P)-\ln F(M(P),P)$ on
$[\Phat,\infty)$. Since $\ln F=-\eta_F\ln(M/\bar M)+\ln[(1-\omega)e+\omega v(P)]$,
\[
\frac{\dd\Psi}{\dd\ln P}=(1-\theta_F)\,\eps_v-\eta_F\,\frac{n\,D}{n+\xi/s}<0\quad\text{for }P>\Phat,
\]
because $\eps_v<0$ there and $\theta_F<1$. $\Psi(\Phat)>0$ by assumption. As
$P\to\infty$, $v\to0$ while $F\ge(1-\omega)e\,(M/\bar M)^{-\eta_F}$ is bounded
away from zero when $e>0$, so $\Psi<0$ eventually. Hence there is a unique root
$P^*>\Phat$. Crossings on $(0,\Phat)$ violate stability, since there $v'>0$.
Output per researcher is $aF$ by \cref{lem:little}, and $F=v(P^*)<\vhat$. With
$\eta_F=0$, $v(P^*)=(1-\omega)e+\omega v(P^*)$, so $v(P^*)=e$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:volume}.} In the large-topic limit $M=N/n(P)$,
with members per topic $n(P)$ from \cref{lem:career}, which are proportional to
$1/a$. So the equilibrium condition $w(P)=F(N/n(P),P)$ involves $N$ and $a$
only through $Na$, and
$\dd\ln M=\dd\ln(Na)-D\,\dd\ln P$, where
\[
D=\frac{\dd\ln[n\,a]}{\dd\ln P}=1-\eps_x+\frac\rho\delta\,\frac{P/b_c}{1-P/b_c}.
\]
Log-differentiating,
\[
\eps_w\,\dd\ln P=\theta_F\eps_U\,\dd\ln P-\eta_F\,\big[\dd\ln(Na)-D\,\dd\ln P\big],
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:E} since $\eps_w<0$. Then $\dd\ln M=\dd\ln N-D\,\dd\ln P$,
and papers per researcher $a\,x^*(P)$ move with elasticity $\eps_x\,\mathcal E$. If
$\eta_F=0$, $P^*$ solves $w(P)=(1-\omega)e+\omega\bar U_F(P)$, which involves
neither $N$ nor $a$. The overlap rate and the aware share are functions of $P^*$
by \cref{lem:credit}. Free entry into science changes $P^*$ only through $N$.
Outside the large-topic limit, $N=M\,(n+\xi/s)$ and $D$ is multiplied by the
share of researchers who are members, $n/(n+\xi/s)$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:planner}.} (i) In the large-topic limit every
spell a researcher takes is a spell in a topic of the common density $P$,
worth $a\,w(P)$ by \cref{lem:career}, so output per researcher is $a\,w(P)$ by
the renewal-reward argument of \cref{lem:little}. Its maximiser is $\hat P_w$,
unique by strict log-concavity of $w$ (\cref{prop:mature}(i); with $b_c=\infty$,
$w=v$ and $\hat P_w=\Phat$). Since $\hat P_w<P^*$, the planner has fewer members
per topic and, with $N$ fixed, more topics. (ii) With $\omega=0$ and $\eta_F=0$,
$E=kM$ with $k=\xi/s$, and output is $a\,M\,(e\,k+n\,w)$ with $M=N/(n+k)$.
Maximising $(ek+nw(P(n)))/(n+k)$ over $n$, the first-order condition is
$(w+n\,w_n)(n+k)=ek+nw$, or $k(w-e)=-n\,w_n\,(n+k)$. Since
$n\,w_n/w=\eps_w/D$, this is \eqref{eq:plannerfoc}. At $P^*$, $w=F=e$, so the
left side of \eqref{eq:plannerfoc} is zero while the right side is positive:
the planner lowers density. At $\hat P_w$ the right side is zero while the left
side is positive when $\hat w>e$: she raises it. So $P^o\in(\hat P_w,P^*)$.
(iii) With a subsidy $s^o$ to explorers the equilibrium condition becomes
$w(P)=F(M,P)+s^o$, which $s^o=\hat w-F(M^o,\hat P_w)$ makes hold at $\hat P_w$.
(iv) Let the planner also set $x$. Per-member output is
$a\,g(x,P)\,(1-P/b_c)^{\rho/\delta}$ and, at fixed membership,
$\partial\ln P/\partial\ln x=1/D_m$ with
$D_m=1+(\rho/\delta)(P/b_c)/(1-P/b_c)$. The first-order condition for $x$ is
\[
\partial_x\ln g+\big[\partial_P\ln g+(\ln\Gamma)'(P)\big]\frac{P}{x\,D_m}=0,
\]
where we use the newcomer's survival factor for the value of membership. At the
private optimum $\partial_x\ln g=0$ and, by the envelope theorem,
$\partial_P\ln g+(\ln\Gamma)'(P)=(\ln w)'(P)$. The wedge therefore has the sign
of $(\ln w)'$, and it vanishes at $\hat P_w$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:mature}.} (i) $\ln\Gamma=\nu\ln(1-P/b_c)$ has
second derivative $-\nu/(b_c-P)^2<0$, so $\ln w=\ln v+\ln\Gamma$ is strictly
concave on $(0,b_c)$, and it tends to $-\infty$ at $b_c$. Since
$(\ln w)'(\Phat)=(\ln\Gamma)'(\Phat)<0$, the peak of $w$ lies below $\Phat$
when $\Phat<b_c$. (ii) The equilibrium condition and stability are those of
\cref{def:eq}; $w(b_c)=0<F$ puts the falling-side root below $b_c$. The premium
follows from $v(P^m)\Gamma(P^m)=F$ and $-\ln\Gamma=(\rho+\xi)T$. (iii) If
$\Phat<b_c$, $P^m<\Phat$ iff $w(\Phat)<F$, since $w$ is falling on
$(\hat P_w,b_c)$; this reads $\ln\vhat+\nu\ln(1-\Phat/b_c)<\ln F$. If $\Phat\ge
b_c$, $P^m<b_c\le\Phat$. (iv) From $\ln\zeta+\ln v(P^m)+\ln\Gamma(P^m)=\ln F$,
differentiate with respect to $\ln P^m$. With $x=P^m/b_c$, the function
$x/(1-x)+\ln(1-x)$ is zero at $x=0$ and has derivative $x/(1-x)^2>0$, so
$\eps_\Gamma>-\ln\Gamma$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{thm:speed}.} (i) By \cref{lem:career}, $T=\infty$ for
any positive stock when $P\ge b_c$. The value of joining is then zero whatever
$v(P)$ is, so no newcomer joins and incumbents' payoffs are not competed away.
(ii) $w$ is continuous and positive on $[0,b_c)$ with $w(0)=1$ and $w\to0$ as
$P\to b_c$. From $\zeta w(P^m(\zeta))=F$, $w(P^m)=F/\zeta\to0$ as
$\zeta\to\infty$, which forces $P^m(\zeta)\to b_c$. With $x=P^m/b_c\to1$, the
premium $-\nu\ln(1-x)$ and the gradient $\nu x/(1-x)$ diverge, and their ratio
$x/[-(1-x)\ln(1-x)]\to\infty$. (iii) Along the growth path the stock is
positive for $P>P_0$, so the myopic value
$v(P)[1+\delta S/(b_c-P)]^{-\nu}$ tends to zero as $P\to b_c$; entry stops at
a density where it equals $F>0$, below $b_c$. (iv) With fixed membership and
reading shares, \eqref{eq:flow} scales every flow by $1+\alpha$. A topic whose
new flow is at least $b_c$ is closed by (i). With no entry, membership falls
at rate $\rho$, and the flow falls at the same rate at fixed reading shares, so
it stays at or above $b_c$ for $\ln[(1+\alpha)P^m/b_c]/\rho$ periods. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{cor:overcrowd}.} Total member output in a topic is
$n\,a\,w(P(n))$, so one more member changes the output of the others by
$n\,a\,w'(P)\,\dd P/\dd n$. Here $\dd P/\dd n>0$ and $w'<0$ at a stable
equilibrium. Writing $w'=v'\Gamma+v\Gamma'$ separates the spillover on
incumbents from the catch-up burden. The ordering $\hat P_w<P^m<\Phat$ combines
\cref{prop:mature}(iii) with stability. The loss follows from
\cref{prop:planner}: per-researcher output is $aF$ in equilibrium and $a\hat w$
at the optimum. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:social}.} (i) At private choices, $\ln s(P)=\ln g(r^*(P),P)-\theta P[(\lambda_s-\lambda)-(\Delta_s-\Delta)\varphi(br^*/P)]$. Differentiate in $P$. The derivative of $\ln g(r^*(P),P)$ is $v'/v$ by the envelope theorem. The direct derivative of the bracket term is $-\theta[(\lambda_s-\lambda)-(\Delta_s-\Delta)(\varphi-c\varphi')]$. Because researchers do not maximise $\ln s$, the bracket term also changes through $r^*$, by $\theta b(\Delta_s-\Delta)\varphi'(c)\,r^{*\prime}(P)$. The effect of one more member on others' social output is $n\,a\,s_w'(P)\,\dd P/\dd n$, as in \cref{cor:overcrowd}. (ii) With $\Delta_s=\Delta$ the bracket term is $-\theta(\lambda_s-\lambda)P$, independent of $r$, so $s=ve^{-\theta(\lambda_s-\lambda)P}$ and $s_w=we^{-\theta(\lambda_s-\lambda)P}$. In the large-topic limit the social planner maximises $s_w$, which is strictly log-concave. Its peak lies below $P^m$ if and only if $(\ln s_w)'(P^m)<0$, that is $(\ln w)'(P^m)<\theta(\lambda_s-\lambda)=-\tau\theta\lambda$. Since $(\ln w)'(P^m)=\eps_w(P^m)/P^m<0$, this is $\tau<|\eps_w(P^m)|/(\theta\lambda P^m)$. (iii) With $\ln C_s=(1-\tau)\ln C$, $\ln s=\ln v-\tau\ln C^*$, and the planner maximises $\ln s+\ln\Gamma$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{thm:incidence}.} Index the tool by $\tau$, so that
$w=w(P;\tau)$, $F=F(M,P;\tau)$ and $n=n(P;\tau)$. The equilibrium condition is
$\ln w=\ln F$, and in the large-topic limit $M=N/n$. Let $h=\partial_\tau\ln w$ and $m=\partial_\tau\ln n$ at
fixed $P$. Let $f$ be the derivative of $\ln F$ in $\tau$ at fixed $P$ when $M$
follows $N/n(P;\tau)$; it is the direct effect plus $\eta_Fm$. Differentiating,
\[
\eps_w\,\dd\ln P+h=f+\theta_F\eps_U\,\dd\ln P+\eta_FD\,\dd\ln P,
\]
so $\dd\ln P=(h-f)/\Psi$. By \cref{lem:little}, $Y/N=aF$, and in equilibrium the
total change in $\ln F$ is $f+(\theta_F\eps_U+\eta_FD)\,\dd\ln P=f+(\Psi-|\eps_w|)(h-f)/\Psi$,
which gives \eqref{eq:incidence}. For \eqref{eq:passthrough}, $\mathcal E=\eta_F/\Psi$
by \cref{prop:volume}, so $\Psi(1-\mathcal ED)=|\eps_w|+\theta_F\eps_v$ and
$\lambda_F=(1-\mathcal ED)|\eps_w|/(|\eps_w|+\theta_F\eps_v)$. When mature topics lie on
the rising side of $v$ ($\eps_v\ge0$), the fraction is at most one and
$1-\lambda_F\ge\mathcal ED$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{cor:ordering}.} (i) $a$ does not enter $v$ or $\Gamma$,
so $h=0$, and $n\propto1/a$ at fixed $P$, so $m=-\dd\ln a$ and $f=-\eta_F\,\dd\ln a$.
Then $\dd\ln(Y/N)=\dd\ln a\,(1-\lambda_F\eta_F)$, and $\lambda_F\eta_F=\mathcal E|\eps_w|$.
(ii) A learning tool raises $\ln v$ by $h_v$ at every $P$, hence $\ln w$ by $h_v$
and $\ln\bar U_F=\ln v(P)$ by $h_v$, so the direct effect on $\ln F$ is
$\theta_Fh_v$; $m=-\partial_\tau\ln x^*$ is second order for the tools we
consider. Then $\Pi_L=\lambda_F\theta_F+1-\lambda_F$. (iii) A catch-up tool raises
$\ln\Gamma$ only; founders' payoffs do not involve $\Gamma$, so the direct effect on
$F$ is zero and $\Pi_C=1-\lambda_F$. (iv) $\Pi_L-\Pi_C=\lambda_F\theta_F\ge0$ and
$1-\mathcal E|\eps_w|-\Pi_L=\lambda_F(1-\theta_F-\eta_F)$. The per-hour statement combines
these with \cref{lem:timeshares}: reading saves $r^*\dd\ln b$ of time and is worth
$\Pi_L\,r^*\dd\ln b/x^*$ in the long run; production saves $x^*\dd\ln a$ and is
worth $(1-\mathcal E|\eps_w|)\,\dd\ln a$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:ai}.} (i) and (ii) restate \cref{prop:volume} and
\cref{cor:ordering}(i): $P^*$ depends on $Na$; output per researcher is $aF$;
papers per researcher are $a\,x^*(P^*)$. Under free entry into science
$aF=u_0+c$. For (iii)--(iv), $\partial_b\ln g=r[\partial_R\ln Q+\partial_R\ln C]\ge0$.
$Q$ is increasing in $\beta$. $C$ is increasing in $\sigma$ and decreasing in $\ell$,
and $\ell$ is increasing in $\kappa_p$. $\Gamma$ is increasing in $b_c$. So $w$ rises
pointwise by $h$ while $F$ rises by at most $\theta_Fh<h$, and \cref{thm:incidence}
gives $\dd\ln P^*>0$; $M^*=N/n(P^*)$ falls because $n$ is increasing. The speed
limit is $b_c$ itself. The overshooting threshold falls with $b_c$ because
$g^*_\infty$ in \eqref{eq:gstar} is decreasing in $b_c$ and the myopic value on the
growth path is increasing in $b_c$ at every $(P,S)$. (v) $A$ in \eqref{eq:aware}
is increasing in $\varphi$, and $\varphi$ is increasing in $\sigma$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:audience}.} In a topic, $n_p$ producing members
read $n_p\,b\,r^*$ papers per period and write $P=n_p\,a\,x^*$, so reads per
paper are $\mathcal A=(b/a)\,r^*/(1-r^*)$. Then
$\ln Y_\alpha=\ln Y+\alpha\ln\mathcal A$. For a production tool,
$\dd\ln(Y/N)=(1-\mathcal E|\eps_w|)\,\dd\ln a$ by \cref{cor:ordering} and
$\dd\ln\mathcal A=-\dd\ln a+\eps_r\mathcal E\,\dd\ln a$ with
$\eps_r=\dd\ln[r^*/(1-r^*)]/\dd\ln P$, so with $\alpha=1$ the total is
$\mathcal E(\eps_r-|\eps_w|)\,\dd\ln a$, a product of small numbers. For a reading
tool, $\dd\ln\mathcal A=\dd\ln b$ plus the change in $\ln[r^*/(1-r^*)]$, and
$\dd\ln(Y/N)=\Pi_L\,r^*\dd\ln b/x^*$ is small when reading takes little time. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{rem:shortrun}.} Let the cross-section of topics have
researcher weights $\omega(a)$ over ages $a$, so that the measured log density is
$L=\int\omega(a)\ln P(a)\,\dd a$. If the area's growth rate rises by $\dd g$ for
$T$ years, the cohorts born in the window are larger by the factor
$e^{\dd g(T-a)}$ relative to older ones, so $\omega$ becomes
$\omega(a)e^{\dd g(T-a)^+}/\int\omega e^{\dd g(T-a)^+}$. To first order,
$\dd L=\dd g\operatorname{Cov}_\omega(\ln P,(T-a)^+)$. Log volume rises by
$T\dd g$ more than it would have, so the composition term in the measured
elasticity is $\operatorname{Cov}_\omega(\ln P,(T-a)^+)/T$. Mature densities
depend on $N$ only through the frontier and adjust at once, which gives
\eqref{eq:shortrun}. A constant growth rate gives a constant age composition,
which area fixed effects absorb. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:toofast}.} On the growth path,
\[
S(t)=\int_0^tP_0e^{gu}e^{-\delta(t-u)}\,\dd u=P(t)\,\frac{1-e^{-(g+\delta)t}}{g+\delta},
\]
and $e^{-(g+\delta)t}=(P_0/P)^{(g+\delta)/g}$, which gives \eqref{eq:growthstock}.
At a given $P$, $S$ is decreasing in $g$: both $1/(g+\delta)$ and
$1-(P_0/P)^{1+\delta/g}$ fall as $g$ rises.

(i) The myopic value $V(P;g)=v(P)[1+\delta S(P;g)/(b_c-P)]^{-\nu}$ is increasing in
$g$ at every $P\in(P_0,b_c)$, so the first $P$ at which it falls to $F$ is
nondecreasing in $g$; it is below $b_c$ by \cref{thm:speed}(iii). Entry starts
because $V(P_0;g)=v(P_0)>F$. As $g\to0$, $S(P;g)\to P/\delta$ uniformly on compact
subsets of $(P_0,b_c)$, so $V\to w$; if $w(P_0)<F$, $w<F$ on $(P_0,P_\ell)$, where
$P_\ell<P^m$ is the rising-side root of $w=F$, and slow topics stop in that
interval. As $g\to\infty$, $S\to0$ and $V\to v$, which exceeds $F$ on
$[P_0,\Pbar_F)$ with $\Pbar_F>P^m$, so fast topics pass $P^m$. By monotonicity
there is a threshold $g_0$ between the two. If entry stops at $P_s<P^m$, then
$P_s<P_\ell$: on $[P_\ell,P^m]$, $w\ge F$ and $V>w$ because the stock along the
path is below the stationary stock. (After such a stop the flow falls at rate
$\rho<\delta$ and the ratio of stock to flow rises towards
$1/(\delta-\rho)>1/\delta$; we verify numerically that, for the calibrated
parameters, the value of joining never returns to $F$.)

(ii) The topic overshoots iff $V(\cdot;g)>F$ on $[P_0,\Phat]$. The set of such $g$
is an upper set by monotonicity, so overshooting is characterised by a
threshold $g^*$. It requires taking off ($g\ge g_0$) and $V(\Phat;g)>F$
($g>g_{\hat P}$). Conversely, if $g>\max\{g_0,g_{\hat P}\}$, $V>F$ on
$[P_0,P^m]$ and at $\Phat$; on $[P^m,\Phat]$, $\ln V$ is concave in $P$ when the
stock is $P/(g+\delta)$, since $\ln v$ is concave and
$-\ln[1+\delta P/((g+\delta)(b_c-P))]=-\ln[b_c-(1-c)P]+\ln(b_c-P)$ with
$c=\delta/(g+\delta)<1$ is concave, so $V>F$ on the whole interval; with the
exact stock we verify this numerically for the calibrated parameters. Hence
$g^*=\max\{g_0,g_{\hat P}\}$. Because the exact stock is below $P/(g+\delta)$,
$V(\Phat;g)$ exceeds its value at the bound, so $g_{\hat P}$ is at most the rate
that solves $\vhat[1+\delta\Phat/((g+\delta)(b_c-\Phat))]^{-\nu}=F$, which is
\eqref{eq:gstar}.

(iii) Suppose entry stops at $P_s\in[P^m,\Phat]$. Afterwards the flow falls
from $P_s$ to $P^m$, so a newcomer at $t_s$ faces flows in $[P^m,\Phat]$, where
$v\ge v(P^m)$, and reads less than she would at the constant flow $P_s$: her
catch-up time is at most $T(P_s,S_s)\le T(\Phat,\Phat/(g+\delta))$, since $T$ is
increasing in both arguments and $S_s\le S(\Phat)\le\Phat/(g+\delta)$. Her value is
at least $v(P^m)\,[1+\delta\Phat/((g+\delta)(b_c-\Phat))]^{-\nu}$. A newcomer to the
mature topic earns $F=v(P^m)(1-P^m/b_c)^\nu=v(P^m)[1+P^m/(b_c-P^m)]^{-\nu}$. If
$g>g^*_{RE}$, then $\delta\Phat/((g+\delta)(b_c-\Phat))<P^m/(b_c-P^m)$, so the
newcomer at $t_s$ earns more than $F$, contradicting that entry stops. Hence
$P_s>\Phat$.

(iv) Incumbents have already paid the catch-up cost. At the stopping date a
newcomer's value equals $F$ after a survival factor below one, so an
incumbent's continuation value exceeds $F$, and incumbents stay. With no entry,
membership decays at rate $\rho$. Using the stationary stock, entry resumes
when $w(P)=F$, at $P^m$. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:sot}.} By \cref{lem:little}, $Y=NaF$, and in
equilibrium $\ln F=\ln w(P^*)$, so $\dd\ln Y/\dd\ln N=1+\eps_w\mathcal E$. Holding $M$
fixed, $N=M\,n(P)$ gives $\dd\ln P=\dd\ln N/D$, and output per researcher is
$a\,w(P)$ by the renewal-reward argument, so
$\partial\ln Y/\partial\ln N|_M=1+\eps_w/D$. The bound with fertility depletion is
proved in \cref{app:fertility}. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of \cref{prop:policy}.} (i) The first follower of a newborn
topic reads nothing and earns $v(P_0)$; she joins only if this exceeds the value
of exploring, $F+s$, or, with a flat entry tax, if $v(P_0)-s>F$. (ii) The tax
$\tau S$ is zero at birth, so (i) does not apply. A newcomer to a stationary topic
pays $\tau P/\delta$, so mature topics solve $w(P)-\tau P/\delta=F$ on the falling
side of the left-hand side, whose peak lies below $\hat P_w$ because the tax
rises with $P$. At $\tau^o$ the equation holds at $\hat P_w$. \qed

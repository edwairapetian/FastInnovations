\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Is innovation too fast? The model gives the question two precise meanings. The
first is a speed limit. A literature can grow faster than the people outside it
can read, and when it does, newcomers cannot join it, however rewarding it is
for those inside. Near the limit, rents rise steeply; a sudden rise in
production speed can push the densest topics past it; and because the stock of
literature lags its flow, topics that grow fast overshoot their best density
even when newcomers see it coming, while topics that grow too slowly never take
off. The second meaning is the one the debate usually has in mind: whether
papers arrive faster than science can absorb them. Here the answer is that
researchers do not pile into the same topics until duplication explodes. They
spread out, and the duplication rate stays where entry pins it. The arXiv record
bears this out. Volume more than doubled while close overlap barely moved, and
the entry margin responds to its cost.

The policy-relevant result is an incidence principle. In the long run, output
per researcher equals the value of opening a new topic, so a research tool is
worth what it adds to that value, not what it adds to the topics that already
exist. Inside a topic, every tool is worth the time it saves. In equilibrium,
tools that speed up production keep almost all of their value; tools that speed
up reading keep the part that reaches founders who stay in the topics they open;
tools that speed up catching up keep the least. The asymmetry is no longer an
assumption about pioneers but a measurable object: the share of the frontier's
value that founders earn as incumbents, bounded below by the volume elasticity
of density. Valued by readership rather than by credited output, the ranking
reverses, which is a reminder that ``scaling science'' is a statement about
units.

Several limits deserve emphasis. Credit is measured by citations, which also
reward audience, so the rising arm of the returns profile is an upper bound on
the catch-up channel. The founders' share is not yet measured, and the
pass-through of reading tools depends on it. The causal test of the detection
channel is a bounded null for passive visibility. It points to search costs as
the right shock, and we have stated that design in advance rather than run it.
The quasi-experiments on entry and catch-up costs are, so far, first stages with
early reduced forms. And the idea space is coarse: topics are cells of fixed
size, nested in fields. A continuous idea space in which topics form
endogenously, with a wide learning kernel and a narrow covering kernel, is the
natural next step, and it would turn the radius pattern of the volume
elasticity from a fact into a test.
